## Proposed scope to decide

- **Site code:** Keep the existing MIT license for layouts, CSS, scripts, and
  other code. Check that the repository's `LICENSE` and README state the scope
  consistently; the current MIT text also mentions associated documentation.
- **Original writing:** Decide whether to reserve reuse rights by default or
  grant an explicit license (for example, CC BY 4.0). Publicly readable does
  not by itself mean freely reusable.
- **Original doodles, photographs, and other media:** Decide which are part of
  the site's identity and whether any should have separate reuse terms.
- **Third-party material:** Identify its source and terms; do not claim rights
  to material you did not make.
- **Older versions:** Consider whether past repository releases granted
  permissions that should be acknowledged rather than silently redefined.

After those decisions, replace this checklist with concise, plain-language
terms and make the footer and repository documentation agree with them.

## privacy

**Design placeholder — not a published privacy policy. Verify before merging.**

- What data does the hosting provider or CDN receive in ordinary requests,
  including IP addresses and access logs? Which provider actually serves the
  deployed site, and what are its retention and processing practices?
- Are analytics, cookies, local storage, embedded media, third-party scripts,
  forms, or comments present on the deployed site? Check the real response,
  not just repository configuration.
- Do RSS subscriptions or outbound links send readers to other services?
  Explain any relevant third-party handling without claiming control over it.
- If a contact route is added, what information does it collect and how long
  is it kept?
- Who should readers contact about privacy questions, and which legal
  requirements apply to the site's audience and location?

Write only what you have verified. If a formal privacy notice is unnecessary,
this draft can be removed instead of published.
